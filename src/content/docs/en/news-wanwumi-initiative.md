Someone once asked: "Besides Phientist, is there a group for humanities enthusiasts too?"

Others have wondered: "Why does the Phientist team also have interest sections? Why do they include chess, music, and other interests?"

Why does this happen?

## 1. Why does this happen?

As children, we were intensely curious about everything unknown. Why? Because it was fascinating — all of it.

Whether it was the beauty of science and engineering or something else, it began as pure affection, and then guided us onto an unknown path.

Some wanted to become doctors, engineers, or scientists. Some wavered, doubting whether they could do it. Some found the material harder and more painful as they went on, and then felt the thrill of conquering it. Love, pain, and accomplishment wove together into the things we love — and into who we are now.

## 2. Beyond the ideal, there is reality

In reality, love is often squeezed by utilitarianism: grades, rankings, GPA, personal connections and nepotism. Good resources are monopolized by one side, and the right to speak is held by a few.

After finishing all this and truly entering society, we still ask: was it necessary? Did it mean anything?

Many people are told: get into a good university, choose a good major, and you can enter the industry you love and do that job for life. But when they actually enter, they find the promised path was beautified. Whether alienated by capital or by power, it is ultimately not as pure as what we imagined as children.

Many others spend enormous enthusiasm on grades and rankings for the entrance exams. When they look back at what they love, they wonder whether they deserve to study it, and feel discouraged because the reality falls so far short of the self they imagined.

## 3. When ideal meets reality

The intertwining of ideal and reality, love and pain, is something every enthusiast goes through. Whether in STEM or not, we ask: is the meaning of sport worth it? Is this choice necessary? Did the patience we struggled through still mean anything?

So I believe we should bring enthusiasts together. Whether or not they are in STEM, they can rediscover their interests here and keep them alive; give every enthusiast a voice, close information gaps, and let all knowledge be shared.

## 4. What is the Wanwumi Series?

This idea grows out of Phientist's philosophy and goals: the Wanwumi Series.

Wanwumi includes all individuals, organizations, research institutes, and enterprises across every industry. It connects them with one another so that each side can gain something and have its needs met.

It will also cross the boundaries of geography and field, letting enthusiasts around the world share this celebration of love, exchange ideas, and build connections.

## 5. Why is Phientist initiating it?

This is a huge goal and a great ideal. Such a world cannot be built by "us" alone.

Since Phientist set sail again in 2024, the team has been involved in dozens of communities and now has over a thousand members.

It must be acknowledged that Phientist is a team focused on STEM, made up of thousands of science and technology enthusiasts since 2014, and that has never changed. Beyond STEM, we no longer have the energy to spare.

But we believe every group of enthusiasts deserves its own community to speak for what it loves. That is why the Phientist team has always held to openness, sharing, cooperation, and inclusiveness.

We will provide the platform. Its overall operations, development, and maintenance will be handled entirely by the team.

## 6. Build Wanwumi together

We remain open to every cooperation proposal. Every enthusiast, organization (including civil organizations), enterprise, and research institute can speak on the platform and help build it — turning it from an idea into reality, and bringing the future into view.

> Love all things, share all things! Build Wanwumi together!
