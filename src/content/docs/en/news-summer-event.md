## Why Do This Every Year

Doing something once is different from making something people want to come back to. The first
summer camp showed what keeps people in — subject mini-sessions, an ask-anything slot, linked
mini-challenges — and what should be dropped.

This summer, the Event Planning Office keeps the parts that worked, trims the slow parts, and
makes sign-up and updates straightforward, so arriving isn't guessing where and when to show up.

## The Rough Shape This Year

The plan converges on three tracks:

- **Subject talks and seminars**: take one concrete question seriously, leave time for questions
  and further reading.
- **Breakout discussions**: small rooms built around a topic, where everyone can speak rather
  than only listen.
- **Optional warm-ups**: a low-pressure way in for first-time participants, with no requirement
  to join.

All three tracks share one schedule and one registration channel, so nobody has to guess where to
sign up. Themes, times, and participation details are still being finalized; once they settle, we
will publish them together.

## What Planning Is Working On

"In planning" is real work. The current tasks are concrete:

- Finalizing themes: each session needs a clear question and an output, not just a title.
- Assigning volunteers: hosts, note-takers, question wranglers, and technical support each have
  an owner.
- Registration: what to ask, when to close, and whether to cap attendance, written down in
  advance.
- Updates: announcements, group messages, and page updates use the same timetable.

Until those line up, we would rather not publish half a timetable and make people check
repeatedly.

## How to Prepare

If you plan to join, think of one question you are genuinely curious about and bring it. For
technical sessions, install the required software in advance. If you only want to listen, that
is fine too.

You do not need to be "expert enough" for a first event. The design goal is to give people at
different levels an entry point, not to filter them.

## How to Help

The Event Planning Office is still looking for volunteers. You do not have to plan an entire
event; owning one small piece — taking notes at a meeting, tidying a sign-up sheet, answering
questions for an hour on the day — is already useful. Contact the office in an official group if
you are interested.
